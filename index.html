About me

I work on safe and feasible adaptive control: keeping uncertain machines inside their limits.

I'm a Research Associate in the Formal Control and Autonomous Systems (FOCAS) Lab, Department of Cyber-Physical Systems, IISc Bengaluru, working with Dr. Pushpak Jagtap on safe control and robotic autonomy.

I earned my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering at IIT Delhi (2026) under Prof. Shubhendu Bhasin as a Prime Minister's Research Fellow, after a B.Tech. in Electrical Engineering at NIT Durgapur. My work makes controllers respect state, input and input-rate constraints, with feasibility guarantees you can verify ahead of time.

Beyond the equations, I'm behind a camera, catching the stories that happen in quiet moments.

02

My research, explained with a car

uncertain system
i · the problem

Driving on ice, in a car you've never tested.

You don't know exactly how the engine responds. Engineers call this an uncertain system. An adaptive controller learns those unknowns while it drives.

ii · state constraints

Now add guard rails.

Position and speed must stay within limits. I use barrier Lyapunov functions: an energy-like quantity that shoots to infinity at the rail. The closer the car gets, the harder the controller pushes back, so it never touches the rail.

iii · input constraints

The pedal only goes so far.

Real motors saturate. Many textbook guarantees quietly assume they don't. My controllers respect the actuator limit and treat the shortfall as something the stability proof has to absorb.

iv · time-varying constraints

Then the rails close in.

Early on, wandering is fine. Later the lane is tight. The limits become a funnel that shrinks over time, and the car has to stay inside the whole way, with a shrinking pedal budget too.

v · feasibility

Before you start: is the engine strong enough?

Ask for limits that are too tight and no controller can meet them. My feasibility conditions are simple inequalities that tell you before any test drive whether your limits can be met at all.
